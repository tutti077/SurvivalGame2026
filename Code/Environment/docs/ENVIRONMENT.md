## Wind, grass, tree wind, chopping, traps (`Code/Environment/`)

- **Grass blade texture** (2026-11-23): the clump models carry UVs (u across the blade, v up it, two tiles per tuft height) and `grass_blade.shader` multiplies `TextureDetail` (`grass_blade_detail.png`, 128 px, point sampled, x2 so linear 0.5 is neutral) over Base -> Tip: lengthwise fibres, a pale midrib with a dark crease, dry nicks. `DetailStrength` 0 turns it off. Both come from `Blender/scripts/create_grass_clump.py`.

- WindSystem (scene wind: host owns heading + base strength via [Sync], every client simulates gusts; publishes WindDirection / WindStrength / WindGust to all shaders through Scene.RenderAttributes once per frame). **Grass gusts are orbs** (2026-11-18, Mark: "it randomly freaks out all together at once, there should be like invisible orbs that float through the fields and make the grass wiggle when it hits them"): up to 8 discs (**Gust Orbs - Orb Count** 6, **Orb Radius Min / Max (m)** 5–12, **Orb Speed (m/s)** 7, **Orb Range (m)** 70, **Orb Life (s)** 10, **Orb Strength** 1) appear round the camera a little upwind, drift downwind (each veers up to ±17°), fade in and out, and are published as `WindOrb0..7` (centre xy + radius in engine units, strength); grass_blade.shader leans and shivers only the clumps inside one. Under them the breeze is a value-noise field sliding downwind by `WindFieldOffset`, which WindSystem accumulates (**Grass Breeze - Breeze Drift (m/s)** 4) — the shader used to multiply the heading by the clock, so the slow heading wander swung the whole field, faster the longer the session ran (that was the "all together at once"). Grass no longer reads the scene-wide `WindGust` (trees and the sea still do). Orbs, drift and orb strength scale with the base wind (the weather); `windtest` makes the orbs bigger and harder. Local per client, like every gust.
- **Weather** = `Code/World/Environment/WeatherSystem.cs` + `Assets/data/weather.json` (2026-11-18, Mark: "clean up the wave and weather system so a thunderstorm would cause massive waves on the ocean"). Before it, "weather" was the wind's base strength (console only) and a sky preset fixed per scene, so the sea never left its everyday swell. The host holds one state (`clear` wind 0.25, `breezy` 0.45, `overcast` 0.4, `thunderstorm` 1.0 under the new `storm` sky preset), synced by id, and picks the next by `weight` when its `minMinutes`–`maxMinutes` are up (**Weather - Auto Cycle**, on). A state only sets the wind (base + gust strength ease over `transitionSeconds` 60) and the sky preset (the cycle cross-fades, **Sky - Preset Blend (s)** 45); everything else follows the wind as it already did: the sea's swell height and wavelength (energy 1 = 2.8× the everyday swell, eased over 40 s), chop, whitecaps, cloud scroll, grass orbs. Console `weather [id|auto|reload]`; `wind_base` only works in a scene without a WeatherSystem. The component sits beside WindSystem in main and terrainTest. No rain, lightning or thunder sound yet.
- GrassPatch + GrassScatterer (grass through the engine Sandbox.Clutter system — GPU instanced/culled/LOD; shader Assets/shaders/grass_blade.shader, models grass_clump1–6_v2 from Blender/scripts/create_grass_clump.py). **v2 tufts** (2026-11-18, Mark: "too similar to each other, kinda the wrong color, too thin and not low poly"): six different plant shapes (broad tuft, tall spray, low rosette, bent tuft with a knee, seed stalks with a faceted head, windswept) built from blades folded along the midrib and standing round the root (24–50 triangles), not single-plane fans. Per clump — hashed from the instance's root position in the shader, because the old vertex-colour B was baked per MODEL — its own height (**Shape - Height Variation** 0.35) and girth (**Width Variation** 0.3), brightness (**Color Variation** 0.3), a drift toward dry straw on some clumps (**Dry Color / Dry Amount** 0.6) and its distance-thinning rank. Faces are two-toned by compass bearing (**Facet Shade** 0.38). Colours sit in the Clover ground tile's palette (Base 0.018/0.045/0.010, Tip 0.050/0.100/0.020); the shader no longer adds indirect specular, which washed the blades out to pale sage.
- World grass = TerrainWorldManager.Grass.cs: ONE ClutterComponent in INFINITE streaming mode (a ring of tiles the engine keeps around the camera — ALWAYS 12 tiles, so Grass — Range (m), default 77 since 2026-11-19, picks the tile size) planted on the loaded chunks' meshed height grids (BuildResult.HeightsZ, bilinear — no physics, no collider, nothing else can be hit); a chunk arriving / remeshing invalidates its tiles; ONE density, grass_blade.shader thins clumps by camera distance (Thin Full 35 / Far 58 / Keep 0.35 / Cull 68 m on grass_blade.vmat - Cull must stay inside the ring; a clump crossing its keep threshold grows out of the ground over ThinGrowBand instead of popping); console grass_toggle; Clover Hills only via an 8×8 biome mask cached per chunk (64 pipeline samples — built on the chunk's sampling worker since 2026-11-04, `PendingChunkBuild.GrassMask`, so a chunk finish no longer spends ~1.2 ms of the frame on it)
- Wind arrow: TerrainMinimapHud draws "wind" + an arrow under the zoom buttons (map orientation, points the way the wind blows, brightens with gusts; hidden without a WindSystem)
- Tree wind = pixel_lit.shader's F_WIND feature (vertex stage, same WindDirection / WindStrength / WindGust inputs as grass_blade): trunk lean + slow rock from object-space height, limb bob / flap from radius off the trunk axis, optional leaf-card rustle — no vertex colours, weights are continuous in position so faceted meshes never tear. Only the STANDING elm gets it: elm_bark_v76_wind.vmat + elm_leaves_v76.vmat (written by create_elm_tree.py); stump, log and halves keep the static bark. Rumple's clover tree (cloverhills/bark.vmat) is deliberately left alone (Mark 2026-10-13). Knobs are metres on the vmat ("Wind" group: Tree Height, Crown Radius, Trunk Sway, Limb Bob, Fade Start / End …). The shader's strength / gust DEFAULTS are 0 and every term is proportional to them (ONE bend per tree, a ROTATION about the root with the angle growing up the trunk — never a stretch — swinging from past upright to 1.4x the lean; limbs rotate about the trunk axis so downwind limbs dip, upwind lift, both compress; WindGust is one scene-wide value so every tree leans together; leaf cards wiggle in their own random direction / rate / phase hashed from the card's face normal, so nothing tears and no two cards jiggle alike; grass gusts are the WindSystem's orbs over a drifting value-noise breeze in grass_blade.shader), so trees only move while a WindSystem is publishing (play mode, scene has one — testscene1 + terrainTest do; WindSystem "Wind Enabled" / console wind_toggle switches it off live; windtest [1-5] = wind perpendicular to the camera (toward its right) + a forced gust of that strength HELD until a plain windtest; 3+ exceeds the normal gust ceiling, 5 = hurricane ×2.5, the usable max — the shader does not cap the gust); the editor viewport is still (Mark 2026-10-12). Sway fades out Fade Start → Fade End metres from the camera (300 → 360 on the elm). Shadow passes must sway identically to the main pass (a cheaper shadow sway self-shadowed the displaced crowns blue) and the vertex stage skips the math past it.
- MineableRock — breakable rocks, sectioned boulders and spires (AGENTS.md *Mineable rocks*); its debris is ChopDebris, the stone drops where the debris comes to rest.
- ChopableTree — two tree types on one component. Type 1 felling (elm): tree → its stump + one universal pointy log that tips over → log splits into two halves → halves / stump drop wood. Type 2 crown fall (clovertree_chop: a disabled "chop_crown" child = one rigidbody whose colliders are Rumple's butt / fork / top "chop_piece" children): tree → stump, the crown tips over away from the chopper (ChopFallingCrown), on landing it splits into the pieces (local ChopDebris, gone after Piece Lifetime) and the host drops the wood there. Plant fiber (Loot - Fiber Drop Min / Max, Mark 2026-11-19) drops once when the standing tree comes down: round the trunk for a felled elm or a sapling, with the wood where a crown lands; Clover Hills trees 4-6, elm saplings 1-2 fiber + 1-2 wood (half and half); stumps, logs and halves drop none. Stages broadcast via WorldScatterIdentity. Models Assets/models/environment/cloverHills/trees/cloverTree/, bark shared from materials/environment/cloverHills/trees/cloverTree), harvest yields, teleport pads
- SunShadowDistance (on the scene Sun: multiplies the engine's cascade shadow reach so the shadow ring is not at the player's feet). 2026-11-23 (Mark: "shadows arent rendering unless I'm right on top of the area"): the component was missing from the Sun in terrainTest and main (only testscene1 had it) and **Vegetation - Tree Shadow Range (m)** was 100, so only trees on chunks within 100 m cast a shadow; now the Sun carries it at **Cascade distance scale** 8 in both scenes and Tree Shadow Range is 330 (every tree object casts one). terrainTest's cycle was a 3-minute loop for a few hours that day and is back at 20 minutes (Day 500 s / Night 500 s / Dawn-dusk blend 100 s each), like main
- BearTrap (hammer-placed trap_small / trap_large: holds players 5 s, animals + enemies 10 s by TrapSize band; hold lives on PlayerMovement.TrapLocked / EntityLocomotion.IsTrapped)
- DamageOverTimeTrap (scene hazard volume)

## Sky and day / night (`Code/World/Environment/`)

- EnvironmentDayNightCycle (sun / moon arc, day counter) feeds SkyDome (camera-centred sphere, shaders/environment/sky_dome.shader: gradient + sun glow + two wind-scrolled cloud layers from sky_clouds.png, Blender/scripts/create_sky_clouds.py) from SkyPresetCatalog (Assets/data/sky_presets.json — per-weather night / dawn / day / noon / dusk keyframes); stars are a procedural layer in sky_dome.shader (StarVisibility from the cycle)
- sky dome + cycle live in lightingTest (3-minute loop, drives the AerialPerspective fog colour), testscene1 (5 min day / 4 min night, no haze) and terrainTest (the lightingTest 3-minute group copied as one "DayNightCycle" object — ENABLED by default since 2026-10-21 (Mark: "it should be running the short daylight cycle") — cycle + sky dome + disks + moon, driving the scene Sun; console cycle_toggle switches it on / off; since 2026-11-03 (Mark: "10am when the terraintest loads … the daylight cycle should be on 20 minutes") a 20-minute loop — Day 500 s / Night 500 s / Dawn-dusk blend 100 s each — starting at **Start hour (0–24)** 10); **Start hour** replaced "Start at night": the sun turns at a constant rate, so hour h is cycle point (h − 6) / 24 (6 = sunrise, 12 = noon, 18 = sunset), default 6 keeps the other scenes at sunrise, and the clock holds while the world load screen is up (`TerrainWorldManager.IsLoadScreenUp`, generating + settling) so the scene opens on the start hour; console cycle_day / cycle_night jump to noon / midnight (the Y / U hotkeys were removed 2026-11-03: the U action fired while Mark flew the fly camera and snapped the world to midnight)
- **Caldera ring** (2026-11-03, Mark: "we live in a caldera … a MASSIVE mountain ring encircles the whole map, 5000 m+ tall, always visible and never cut by view distance, fog or streaming"; then "VERY flat … some ridges lower, or more jutted out toward the player … should feel real not like a wall of rock"): `CalderaRing` on its own "CalderaRing" object at the scene ROOT of terrainTest (+ its ModelRenderer) builds ONE render-only polar heightfield round the world origin — backdrop only (no collider, no streaming, no LOD, no shadows; the generator is untouched, the rim ocean runs to its foot). It must not sit under the DayNightCycle group: `cycle_toggle` switches that group off, which used to take the mountains with it; the ring finds the cycle once at start (enabled or not) and keeps lighting from the root Sun while the group is off. Shape in metres (Shape group): **Foot Radius (m)** 23 000 (the 22.5 km world edge + 500 m), **Range Depth (m)** 11 000 (foot → main crest, ±25 % round the ring), **Saddle Height (m)** 3200 and **Peak Height (m)** 7000 (smooth ~10 km massifs carry the slopes and set the saddles; sharp ~2 km summits sit only on the top, so a peak never runs down the face as a seam), **Ridge Spacing (m)** 3000 and **Ridge Reach (0–1)** 0.9 (ridge arms run down from the crest toward the caldera, each rolling its own reach — some die near the crest, some carry height almost to the foot — and swing sideways up to ~2 spacings on the way down so they read as diagonal ridgelines, not end-on pleats; valleys between them stay low and dip under the sea near the foot as inlets), Seed; knobs and false summits on top, a 900 m apron under the ocean floor, a 6 km back slope. 1536 columns × 47 rows, faceted in the shader. Vertex colour by grade: basalt cliffs with oxidised tuff strata where steep, grey-brown scree on gentle ground, ash on high gentle ground, valleys darker than the arm crests. `shaders/environment/caldera_ring.shader` draws lit rock only — its distance haze is the scene-wide **Aerial perspective** pass below (since 2026-11-08; before that the ring carried its own 35 km haze because the scene GradientFog ended at 12 km and would have fogged it out). Lit per frame from the cycle's sun / moon DirectionalLights (`LightColor`, the sun's `SkyColor` as ambient). Sorting: everything sky-side must clear the ring's back edge (~42.8 km) plus the camera's offset, so terrainTest's SkyDome **Radius (m)** is 80 000 and the cycle's **Disk orbit radius** 55 000 (the disks orbit the world origin) — a dome inside the ring logs a warning; `SkyDome` raises the camera Z Far to cover its radius (never lowers it). At dusk the sun sets behind the rim (the light keeps lighting the world until the sun is down — the ring casts no shadow).
- **Aerial perspective = distance fog** (2026-11-08 haze, rebuilt 2026-11-10 as real fog — Mark: "the atmospheric fog is having the kinda opposite effect … World looks way too bland and you can still see super clearly … make it like real fog and not just ruin the color scheme"): `Code/World/Environment/AerialPerspective.cs` → `shaders/environment/aerial_perspective.shader`, ONE full-screen pass at `Stage.BeforePostProcess` 500 (after the FarTrees ring at −100, before the underwater view at 1000) on whichever camera is live, so ground, grass, trees, far cards, water, entities and the caldera ring sit in the same fog. Exponential height fog: optical depth = distance × air density averaged between the camera's altitude and the point's (sea level 0, 1/e per **Fog - Fog Scale Height (m)** 1200), fog = 1 − exp(−optical / **Fog - Fog Distance (m)** 4500) — a fifth at 1 km, two fifths at 2 km, nearly gone by 10 km at sea level; the caldera foot melts away while its crests (high, thin air) stand out of it. No cap and no desaturation: a thing keeps its own colour and fades into the fog. The fog colour is the sky dome's colour along the same view ray (sky_dome.shader's gradient, sun-side horizon band and halo — no clouds or stars; below the horizon the horizon colour), handed over by the cycle each frame (`Apply(zenith, horizon, sunGlow, sunDir)`), so a fully fogged object IS the sky behind it, warm toward a low sun, dark at night. **Strength (0–1)** 0 switches it off; **Fog Colour (no cycle)** serves scenes without a cycle. Depth is the engine scene depth (`Depth::GetLinear`, the prepass chain) since 2026-11-19: the `GrabDepthTexture` copy of the live depth buffer came back banded and stale in the live viewport (Mark: "all the shaders were like partially visible through it"; an offscreen camera render was clean, which hid it). The FarTrees ring is not in that depth (a command list, never in the prepass), so the pass runs BEFORE it (`BeforePostProcess` −200, ring −100) and the ring fogs itself: `pixel_lit.shader` `RingFog` with the pass's numbers, handed over per draw from `AerialPerspective.TryGetFog` (`RingFogShape` z = 0 by default, so nothing else is touched). Keep the two fog formulas in step. Clear zone (Mark 2026-11-19: "the fog is 0% inside 100m and slowly increases up from there"): **Fog Start (m)** = 100, nothing nearer is fogged and the optical depth counts only the stretch of the ray past it, with the air density averaged from that point, so the fog rises from 0 at 100 m (about 2 % at 200 m, 6 % at 400 m, a fifth at 1.1 km at sea level). Both the pass (`FogStart`) and the far ring (`RingFogShape.w`) use it. Sky test: pixels whose distance ALONG THE RAY is past 0.95 × the SkyDome radius (or 0.9 × Z Far) are sky and untouched — a view-depth test fogged the whole sky outside a cone round the screen centre; a sky pixel next to geometry fogs at its neighbour's distance (anti-aliased crest pixels otherwise drew a dotted dark line along the ring). What it replaced: the 2026-11-08 version desaturated 63 % of the hue by 1.5 km (**Desaturate Distance**, **Max Desaturation**) but hazed only ~10 % by 2 km (**Haze Distance** 20 km) and capped the haze at 90 % (**Max Haze**), so the world went grey yet stayed sharp, and the capped ring foot came out paler than the sky behind it (read as see-through rock); those knobs and **Sun Warmth** are gone. It had itself replaced the scene GradientFog (start 1 km, end 12 km). Lives on the DayNightCycle object; tune it in the inspector during play.
- **Biome air** (Mark 2026-11-23: "it needs to be biome specific" — a light fogginess in Clover Hills, "pretty dark under the trees" and mysterious in Redwood Forest; the first try made the tree count do it, which only bought far too many trees): `Assets/data/biome_atmosphere.json` → `BiomeAtmosphereCatalog` (`Code/World/Environment/BiomeAtmosphere.cs`), one row per biome: `mistDistanceMeters` / `mistStartMeters` / `mistMax` / `mistTint` (a near mist whose colour is the sky fog colour × the tint, so it follows the time of day), `shade` (everything that is not sky is multiplied by it) and `ceilingMeters` (the air ends that high above the ground, fading over 30 m, so a camera above the canopy sees the world as usual). Clover Hills: mist 3600 m, pale, no shade. Redwood Forest: mist 220 m (both doubled the same day — Mark: "the fog is too thick, i want players to see twice as far"; the redwood ambiance itself was accepted), tint 0.16 / 0.22 / 0.22, cap 0.94, shade 0.5, ceiling 95 m. `AerialPerspective.UpdateBiomeAir` samples the land biome weights under the CAMERA four times a second (one `TerrainPreviewPipeline.Sample`), mixes the rows by weight (mist densities add, tint follows density) and eases to the result over `blendSeconds` (4); the same pass applies shade → mist → world fog (`FogMist` / `FogMistShape`), and the FarTrees ring does the same in `pixel_lit.shader` RingFog (`RingFogMist` / `RingFogMistShape`). The sky is never shaded or misted, so canopy gaps stay bright. It is the camera's biome, not the pixel's: from inside the forest the open land beyond is seen through the forest's mist; from outside, the forest is lit as usual. A scene without a `TerrainWorldManager` has no biome air. Console `atmosphere` prints the live mix, `atmosphere reload` re-reads the JSON. With it, **Vegetation - Redwood — Density (0–1)** dropped 0.9 → 0.45, and to 0.225 later the same day.
- **Biome air, second pass the same day** (Mark 2026-11-23, after Clover read "SO white at a distance" and the forest "just illuminated overall"; references `Research/screenshots/valheimBlackForest*.png` - fog coloured by direction, warm toward the sun and saturated blue away from it, a dark rich foreground, sun glare and shafts): (1) **shade is real light** - `AerialPerspective.BiomeShade` scales the sun light's `SkyColor` (the ambient fill) in `EnvironmentDayNightCycle` (half the effect by night); the screen multiply is gone, so shadows go dark and the sun's patches stay bright. (2) **Sun side of the mist** - row value `mistSunTint` x the preset's sun glow colour is added toward the sun (phase `pow(dot * 0.5 + 0.5, 4)`), `mistTint` is the colour away from it. (3) **Colour hold** - `groundColorHold` (0.45, fog pass; 0.65 at first, which turned far hills teal and the dunes into orange bands) and `treeColorHold` (0.25, the FarTrees ring): the world fog's colour is multiplied by part of the fogged thing's own chroma, so far land fades to a blue-green instead of white and holds its colour longer than the far trees (Mark: "the earth material itself should hold more color than trees"); the biome mist itself is not held, silhouettes fade to the air's colour. (4) **Sun shafts** - `sunShafts` (0.5): in the fog pass, each pixel facing the sun walks 24 depth reads toward the sun's place on screen (`FogSunU` / `FogSunV` from `CameraComponent.PointToScreenNormal`) and adds the sun glow colour by how much open sky the walk crossed; off when the sun is down or behind the camera. Every mist number goes to the shaders as its own scalar attribute (`FogMist*`, `RingFogMist*`): the density first rode in a float4's alpha and did not arrive as set. Bloom was already on the camera and is unchanged.
- **Biome air, third pass the same day** (Mark 2026-11-23: "thin out the ambient fog of the whole game ... I want the max foggyness to be decreased"; "the godrays have like a texture to it ... should be like beams"): `fogMax` (0.8, `FogMax` / `RingFogMax`) caps the world distance fog in the pass and on the ring, and the mist caps dropped (`mistMax` 0.5 -> 0.3 Clover, 0.94 -> 0.6 Redwood). The far world therefore never melts fully into the sky: the caldera stays a faint shape (the 2026-11-10 no-cap rule is superseded by this request; raise `fogMax` to 1 to get it back). Sun shafts walk 64 depth reads with a soft sky test instead of 24 hard ones: at 24 neighbouring pixels caught different leaf-card gaps and the shafts speckled.
- **Biome air, fourth pass the same day** (Mark 2026-11-23: "I dont think it should just be a blanket of fog across the whole game. It really dilutes the overall look"): measured on the engine's own viewport captures (console `screenshot`; `camera_screenshot` renders differ) - the fog colour was the sky colour along the ray, ~40x brighter in linear light than foliage, so 5 % of it already doubled the brightness of a forest 1 km away. Now (1) `fogTint` (0.22 / 0.34 / 0.56, `FogTint` / `RingFogTint`): the world fog is that multiple of the sky colour, a deep blue haze - land keeps its darks, the caldera reads as a blue silhouette; (2) Clover Hills has no biome mist (`mistDistanceMeters` 0): open country is clear air; (3) **Fog - Fog Start (m)** 100 -> 300 in terrainTest and main; (4) sun shafts light only what stands in the air, never sky pixels (on open sky they were a white sheet whenever the camera faced the sun); (5) the mist's sun side takes the tint's place toward the sun instead of adding to it (warm over blue was a grey-mauve wash). Side finding, not changed: with the pass on, the sky reads ~20 % darker than with it off even where nothing is fogged.
- **Far view pass, 2026-11-23** (Mark: smoke POI "see it from 1000m away"; far redwoods "resemble the trees in clover hills"; "no fog from 0-100m"; far Redwood ground "feels like a different color"): (1) **crashed pod smoke** - the landmark cluster is `FarRing` (`TerrainWorldManager.Pois.cs`); the far-tree ring plans it out to **Vegetation - Far Trees — Range (m)** (1200) and, for a prefab that carries a `SmokeColumn`, `TerrainWorldManager.FarSmoke.cs` stands a bare object with only that component there (copied from the prefab, `Fade In` on) until the real pod takes over at Tree Objects Range. `SmokeColumn` is a function of its position and `Time.Now`, so the stand-in and the pod's own column are the same column and the hand-over moves no blob (the first version was a static `_far.vmdl` stack: "make the LOD smoke stack move even from far away ... dont make it just pop in, make it fade in"). The live column: Height 100 m, Top Diameter 22 m, 48 blobs, Rise 45 s, tints 0.22 -> 0.8 (prefab and `Blender/scripts/create_crashed_pod.py`). (2) **Conifer far cards** - `TerrainWorldManager.FarImpostors.cs` has a second card template, `BuildFarConiferCardTemplate`: three crossed full-height quads, 0.24 x the height wide, 1 m tall at scale 1, same material and vertex count as the elm card. `far_tree_card.vmat` now reads an atlas (`far_tree_cards_atlas_{color,mask,normal}.png`, 512x256, `Blender/scripts/create_far_card_atlas.py`, plain numpy): the elm card in the left half (u 0-0.5), a whole redwood in the right half drawn from the real materials' mean colours (red bark trunk bare for the lowest third, 17 tiers of drooping dark boughs narrowing to the tip) - the first conifer card was vertex-tinted spikes of the elm crown and visibly swapped at the ring's edge ("the LOD redwood needs to very closely resemble the real redwoods ... change it without players even noticing"). `PlanFarCards` picks the conifer card by prefab name (`environment_redwood*` 90 m, `environment_fir*` 24 m) and skips anything that is not under `/trees/` (the far-ring landmarks). (3) **Fog** - Redwood mist starts at 100 m (was 6) and builds over 350 m; the world fog starts at 300 m; nothing is fogged inside 100 m. (4) **Far Redwood floor** - `ApplyFarCanopyTint` gives Redwood a fixed canopy channel (0.7) and `pixel_lit` darkens Redwood ground toward itself x `SplatRedwoodShade` (0.3) instead of the canopy green: far floor stood bright orange in full sun beside the near floor, which lies in the trees' shadow (far trees cast none).
- **Biome ambiance pass, 2026-11-23** (Mark: one air per biome - Clover Hills "like valheims meadow biome ... thin atmospheric filtering, godrays", Redwood "dark and mysterious ... without whitewashing", Amber Dunes "a dusty feel", Ivory Alps "a baseline of fog ... and then snow that falls in this biome constantly", afloat "same baseline as clover hills", unlisted biomes "the same as clover hills"; "the goal of it is to create depth ... not to remove so much color"): `biome_atmosphere.json` has four rows (CloverHills, RedwoodForest, AmberDunes, Mountain = Ivory Alps); `BiomeAtmosphereCatalog.Mix` gives a biome without a row, open water and anything past the world's edge the CloverHills row. Measured side note: the sky reads ~15 % darker with the fog pass on because auto-exposure answers the brighter hazed frame, not because the pass touches sky pixels.
- **Night and mist floor, 2026-11-23** (Mark: "its far too dark at night in clover hills and redwood, can we add a moon that provides light at night the same as the sun"; "i can see trees 300m away totally fine, try adding like 10% to the fogs strength when it first starts"; "the ambient lighting appears through the player"): the moon light follows the sun's height curve (a fifth at the horizon, full overhead) at **Lighting - Moonlight (vs day)** 0.9 (was 0.34 x height, nothing at the horizon), and the biome shade scales the sky fill by day only. `mistFloor` (0.1 on every row, `FogMistFloor` / `RingFogMistFloor`): the mist comes in at 10 % over the 40 m past its clear distance and builds from there; Clover's cap rose 0.2 -> 0.35 to make room. ## Lighting look (asset-side, Valheim recipe)

- No screen-space pixelation. The look is asset-side (Valheim recipe, Mark 2026-10-02): low-res textures + point filtering + faceted normals baked in the mesh (create_elm_tree.py shade_flat; PixelFlatShading on pixel_lit.shader for meshes that are not), optional point-sampled TextureNormal / TextureRoughness + PixelTexCoordScale on pixel_lit.shader (elm bark v76: colour, normal and roughness all from one height field), then the engine's normal sun/shadows, SSAO, fog, bloom, tonemap. **Per-texel lighting** (pixel_lit `PixelTexelLighting`, default on, Valheim sconce reference 2026-10-05): direct light + shadows are evaluated at the texel centre so each texel is one lit block; `PixelLightSteps` (local lights, default 6 bands — Mark 2026-10-05: torch fall-off must be steppy, not a glow), `PixelLightCutoff` and `PixelSunSteps` (default 0, the sun stays smooth — banding is for fires, torches and lanterns only; a 5-step sun was tried and rejected 2026-10-13) posterise on top. Indirect stays the engine's. Firelight wanders through `Environment/FireLightFlicker` (authored on the campfire prefab's FireLight child, created on the held torch / lantern lamp; wind gusts widen it) — never on the sun. Playground: Assets/scenes/lightingTest.scene (100 m uniformly scaled dev box — non-uniform scale breaks vertex normals — clovertree/whole + fly cam + standard post stack on the camera)
