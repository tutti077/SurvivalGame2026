# Terrain preview — architecture

Source of truth for procedural terrain preview. Read before adding knobs or pipeline stages.

## Mental model

```
Lake mask noise (TerrainPreviewLakeMap)
        ↓
Land disk @ preview÷2
  1. Land biomes + mountains on full land circle (independent of lakes)
  2. Lake mask — quantile threshold + one speck pass (no threshold iteration)
  3. Biome patch merge — single scan on dry land; tiny patches → neighbor biome
        ↓
Land height = base noise → biome sculpt → peaks → rim coast
  • dry land clamped ≥ SeaLevel + margin
        ↓
Open water = exactly SeaLevelMeters (default 0)

**Coastal height:** land near water uses a **soft drain cap** — influence fades in from ~0% at the outer fade edge (2.5× the shore band) to full strength at the water. No hard cutoff at the band radius.
```

**Rules**

- Inland water = speck-filtered mask only (not height valleys).
- All water surfaces are flat at `SeaLevelMeters`.
- Base height noise feeds biome sculpting only.
- Lake straits/islands emerge from noise overlap + shore detail — no artificial breaker pass.

## Biome specks (2026-10-06)

Biome ids come from the arg-max of three scatter noise fields (`TerrainPreviewBiomeResolver.SamplePlacementWeights`), and an arg-max of multi-octave noise throws 30–150 m islands everywhere two fields cross. The land-disk raster (`TerrainPreviewLandDiskFields`, 256–1024 px) always ran `TerrainPreviewBiomeSpeckFilter.MergeSmallPatches` (**Biomes - Min Patch Diameter (m)**, 200 m) — but with **World - Use Continuous Biome Placement** on, the live terrain, trees and biome PNG never looked at that raster. Now the merge records every cell it changed (`TryGetSpeckMerge`: speck biome → surround biome) and the continuous picker drops the speck biome's weight at those points, so everything agrees with the merged raster; Ivory Alps specks merge too (the raster already treated Mountain as mergeable). The merge runs three passes (nested specks). The scatter octaves are also capped so the finest noise feature is at least half the min patch diameter (no change at the defaults). The override is **feathered**: `TryGetSpeckMerge` samples a merged-cell mask bilinearly, so the speck biome fades out and the surround fades in over one cell instead of painting a hard rectangle (first version showed 20 m green squares). Specks smaller than a raster cell are caught by a second rule (`SnapSubCellSpeckToRaster`): where the four cells around the point agree on a dry-land biome and the continuous pick differs, the surround's weight is lifted to the pick's weight — continuous across the speck, no-op at real boundaries where the four cells differ. The merge changes only the sample's `LandWeights` (colour, trees, map) — height shaping keeps the raw continuous weights, because shaping with the merged one-hot weights raised whole raster cells into flat-topped blocks with vertical walls (Mark's screenshot, 2026-10-07).

## Rivers (2026-10-07)

`TerrainPreviewRivers` builds the network once per world inside `TerrainPreviewLandDiskFields.EnsureBuilt`, after lakes and blackwater (~1.3 s on a 20 km world). Routing raster: 256 cells (~80 m) of pre-river dry-land height (`TerrainPreviewPipeline.SampleDryLandHeightMetersNoRivers`), box-smoothed twice. Sources: land above the 70th height percentile, no water within 2 cells, greedily spaced by **Lakes - River Source Spacing (m)**; up to 3× **River Count** are tried. Each river walks to its lowest 8-neighbour (a small penalty against sharp turns), joins an existing river or stops at a lake / the sea; its level falls at least **River Min Grade (%)** per step and never rises above the ground, and a step that would cut more than **River Max Cut (m)** drops the river. Routes are Chaikin-smoothed by **River Route Smoothing (m)**, resampled every ~40 m, levels forced non-increasing and ≥ sea level; width grows √t from **River Width at Source (m)** to **River Width at Mouth (m)**. A fine raster at the land-disk resolution stores the nearest segment per cell; `TerrainPreviewRivers.Sample` refines against ±3 segments. **Carve** (pipeline, after lakes, **cut only** — the first version eased land UP to the water and built levees, Mark 2026-10-07): the water level is **River Depth (m)** (2) below the lowest unsmoothed ground in the 3×3 routing cells around each step, so it always sits under the land it crosses; inside the half width the ground is that water level (the river surface is terrain, flat across and sloped along, like lakes are flat terrain at sea level) and the sample is `RiverWater` (not `IsOnLand`, so no trees); outside, the ground is eased down toward level + 0.2 m over the bank width and never raised (`min(ground, eased)`). The bank width is the larger of **River Bank (× width)** and the local cut depth ÷ tan(**River Bank Max Slope (deg)**, 25°), so a deep cut spreads into a wide slope instead of a canyon. **Level profile from fine ground** (2026-10-07, second pass): the 80 m routing field is box-smoothed and smoothing raises valley floors, so a river routed down a valley at "smoothed minus depth" sat ABOVE the true floor as a raised strip of water with green slopes climbing to it. `SetLevelsFromFineGround` therefore re-derives every route vertex's level from the real pre-river ground at the centre line, both channel edges and the three matching points half way to the next vertex (depth under the lowest), still monotone and >= sea; a route whose cut would exceed 1.6 × **River Max Cut (m)** on the fine ground is dropped. `terrain_river_check [N]` measures water-above-ground per river — use it, not a screenshot. **Water strip winding** (2026-10-07): the per-chunk `RiverWater` quads were wound clockwise seen from above and back-face culled, so the carved channel showed but the water never did; they now wind like the chunk quads (counter-clockwise from above). The bed's chunk vertex colours are water-blue again as well (Mark 2026-10-07: "keep that area blue"), so the channel reads blue from any angle even where the strip is covered; the ground-colour bed variant and its `includeRivers` overload are gone. Known limit seen on river 0: the 80 m routing field can put a route along a hillside shelf, so the ground just beyond one bank is lower than the water — the channel is still cut and holds, but it reads as a river on a ledge; moving routes onto the local valley floor is the next routing pass. `TerrainShorelineDisplay.IsDisplayWaterColor` paints river water blue. Dev: `terrain_tp_river` lists rivers, `terrain_tp_river N` parks the fly camera over river N looking downstream. Not yet: a water mesh / swim volume / flow for boats (Mark wants true sloped rivers to ride a boat on — the surface geometry is in place for that).

## World size 40 km (2026-10-07)

Mark asked for 40 × 40 km. `WorldDiameterMeters` is 40 000 in the settings default, the TerrainWorldManager default, `WorldSaveRecipe`, the terraintest scene and both saved recipes (`WorldSaves/TestWorld/world.json`, `WorldSaves/New World/world.json` in the s&box data folder — the live diameter comes from the recipe when **World - Override World Scalars From Component** is off). Lake wavelengths are metres (unchanged); the biome picker frequency is world-relative, so **Biomes - Patch Frequency** doubled to 60 (slider range raised to 96) to keep patches ~670 m; **Biome groups (2026-10-07, Mark: "still super patchy… it's the octaves in the biome map"):** each land biome's scatter noise was 3 octaves, and the arg-max of three 3-octave fields carved every base patch into 170–330 m sub-patches. **Biomes - Scatter Octaves** is now **1** (one smooth blob per base patch), **Biomes - Patch Frequency** **30** (~1.3 km patches on 40 km) and **Biomes - Min Patch Diameter (m)** **400** so leftover islands merge; both saved recipes carry the same values. **Seeded regions (2026-10-08, Mark: "span many many kilometres, at most 5 sections of each biome"):** noise arg-max can never bound the piece count, so placement is now `TerrainPreviewBiomeResolver.RegionPatch01`: the three land biomes share 3 × **Biomes - Regions Per Biome** (default 4) sites on a golden-angle spiral over the land disc (biomes interleaved, seeded jitter); a point belongs to its nearest site in a frame warped by **Biomes - Region Border Warp (m)** (2000) and the weight crosses 0.5 at the border over **Biomes - Region Edge Blend (m)** (600). Each biome is therefore at most N contiguous pieces of ~10 km. Scatter Octaves / Patch Frequency are legacy-only fields now; the spawn band, distance ramps and the mountain mask still apply on top. **Round two (2026-10-08, Mark: "really blocky… should have 5 of each"):** a plain interleave let neighbouring same-biome sites merge into two blobs, so sites now get their biome greedily (each site takes the biome whose placed sites are farthest, quotas N each — `GetRegionLayout`, cached per seed/count), the warp is two layers (5-cycle swells + 18-cycle wobble, **Region Border Warp (m)** 3500) so pieces are irregular blobs, and **Regions Per Biome** defaults to 5. **Oceans instead of ponds:** Mark wants ~5 big inland seas that may run into the rim: **Lakes - Macro Frequency** 1 → 0.22 (~10 km basins on 40 km), **Lakes - Medium Frequency** 0.4 → 0.15, **Lakes - Target Lake Coverage** 0.10 → 0.16; the land disc already reaches the rim ring so big basins can bleed into it. (`lakeMaxBasinFilter*` / `inlandWaterMinPatchDiameterMeters` in old recipes are legacy fields with no runtime effect.) **Mountain ranges (2026-10-08, Mark: ranges as bold stripes across the map, "not these whispy little things"):** the ridged range field had a 2.2 km macro wavelength, 4 octaves, sharpness 2.2 and a strong breaker, which is exactly thin wisps. Now **Mountain Mask - Macro Wavelength (m)** 6000, **Macro Octaves** 2, **Ridge Sharpness** 1, **Medium Mix** 0.25, **Breaker Strength** 0.1, **Range Stretch** 2.4, **Min Patch Diameter (m)** 600, plus a new **Range Belt Wavelength (m)** 14000 / **Range Belt Threshold** 0.42 plain-FBM gate (`seed + 646`) so ranges have an extent. **Seed:** the component's World Seed is now THE seed (write-through to the recipe); the recipe used to silently replace it, so typing a seed on the component changed nothing. Same seed + changed settings = changed map, by design: Min Patch Diameter 200 → 400 is what moved the mountains on an unchanged biome layout. **Whole-map ranges (2026-10-08):** **Mountain Falloff - Inner Radius (0–1)** 0.3 → 0.075 and **Outer Radius (0–1)** 0.95 → 1 (mountains were confined to a 6–19 km ring = "just a section"), **Range Belt Threshold** 0.36, **Range Belt Wavelength** 20000, **Macro Wavelength** 5000, **Ridge Sharpness** 2, **Range Stretch** 3.5 (the 8000 / sharpness-1 pass gave 4 km wide blobs, Mark 2026-10-08: the crest width is ≈ (1 − threshold^(1/sharpness)) × half the macro wavelength, so sharpness is the width knob and wavelength the spacing). **Rivers everywhere, none in Amber (2026-10-08):** sources were the highest 45 % of land, highest first, which on a ring-of-ranges world put every river in the ring flowing to the rim and none near the centre; now anything above the lowest 30 % qualifies, ordered by a seeded shuffle with a mild height bias, **Lakes - River Source Spacing (m)** 2500. `Build` takes a desert mask from the filtered placement raster: no sources in Amber Dunes and a route that would enter Amber is dropped (`[Rivers] N routes dropped at the Amber Dunes edge`). **River character (2026-10-08, Mark: too straight; twists, varying thickness, little pools):** the routing turn penalty dropped 0.6 → 0.25 so routes follow the ground, and `ShapeRiver` runs after the resample and before the fine-ground level pass: a seeded perpendicular FBM offset (~450 m swings, 1.5–3 channel widths, pinned at source and mouth), a 0.7–1.35 width multiplier (~520 m), and pools where a ~1.4 km FBM peaks (width up to 3.2×, bed depth up to 2.5× — `Segment.DepthA/B` → `Hit.BedDepthMeters` → the pipeline carve). **Regions Per Biome** default 10 (range to 16): ~6 km patches. **Rim trim (2026-10-08):** the thin band around the whole land disc was the Azure Coast biome along the rim ocean (**Biomes - Azure Coast Include Rim Ocean**, 200 m wide); it is off by default and in both recipes, so land ends where the biomes end. Azure Coast still appears on the shores of the inland seas. **Outer band (2026-10-13):** Azure Coast only exists beyond **Biomes - Azure Coast Outer Band Start (0–1)** × the world radius (default 0.7 = the outer 30 %); the old fixed `AzureCoastMinDistanceFromSpawnMeters` (5.5 km, next to spawn on a 40 km world) is a legacy field. **River Count** default 40 (range to 80). Not re-solved: the lake mask offsets (spawn may land near water on the bigger world — run Generate / the spawn solve). The far tier and rasters are size-independent; 40 km is 1.6 M engine units from the centre (float precision ≈ 0.1 u there).

## Rivers, round two (2026-10-07)

**Bed + water mesh**: inside the half width the ground is now a carved bed — a parabola from the water's edge down to **River Bed Depth (m)** (2.5) under the surface at the centre (`min(ground, bed)`, never lifted) — and `TerrainWorldManager.RiverWater.cs` builds one quad per river segment touching a near chunk at the water level (+8 cm) in the terrain vertex-colour material, lake-blue, as a `RiverWater` child of the chunk; sloped along the river, flat across; no collider yet. The bank eases down to the water's edge with no lip. Far-tier chunks carry no water strip.

## Rivers, round three — flowing water + per-biome depth (2026-10-21)

Mark: "sculpt the land so there's a water-slide shape I can fill with water … carve out the riverbed and add flowing water … Clover Hills rivers 4 m deep at most, other biomes gentle for now", and a heads-up that the terrain will become voxels one day (players dig). What changed: **(1) Depth.** `Segment.DepthA/B` now carry a seeded per-river multiplier 0.6–1.2 (`seed + 775`) on top of **River Bed Depth (m)** and the pool multiplier, so some rivers run shallower than others; the pipeline carve clamps the bed depth to a per-biome cap blended on the *continuous* Clover placement weight — **Rivers - River Bed Max Depth, Clover Hills (m)** (4) ↔ **River Bed Max Depth, Other Biomes (m)** (6) — so the cap never steps at a biome border. **Trough** (Mark, same day, after the first pass: "you still haven't carved the riverbed channel"): easing the land straight down to the water's edge over the bank width (≥ 24 m) made a 50 m dish with no visible channel — `terrain_probe 392 -2591 24 2` showed ground 30 → bed 26.7 → 30 m across the whole span, water at 28.4. The cross-section is now water's edge → **Rivers - River Bank Slide Width (m)** (4) of curved wall rising `t²` to a rim **River Bank Height (m)** (1.5) above the water → the old smooth-step bank blend from the rim out to the surrounding ground. Still cut only everywhere (`min(ground, profile)`), so a floodplain already below the rim keeps its own height. `Network.SlideWidthMeters` joins every segment's raster reach and the `Sample` cutoff, and the slide width is in the river fingerprint. `terrain_river_check` now also prints the deepest bed and the rim range above the water per river. The water depth is the bed depth; the cut (water `River Depth` under the lowest surrounding ground) is on top of it. **(2) Water.** The per-chunk `RiverWater` strip was drawn in the terrain vertex-colour material with alpha 1, which is the Clover ground-texture blend — the "water" was a flat green grass strip lying across the channel. It is now `materials/water/riverwater.vmat` → `shaders/riverwater.shader`: tempwater's depth-tinted frame-buffer refraction, with the ripple field in the mesh UVs — u = metres along the river from its source (`Segment.ArcA/ArcB`), v = signed metres across — scrolled downstream at **Rivers - River Flow Speed (m/s)** (1.5), stretched 3× along the flow, plus faint foam streaks (`StreakStrength`). Flow direction is therefore always source → mouth regardless of quad winding. **(3) Swim.** Under the same `RiverWater` child, every segment whose midpoint lies in the chunk gets a `flow N` object: a "water"-tagged `BoxCollider` trigger (length + 1 m, channel width + overlap, from 8 cm above the surface down past the deepest bed of that stretch), rotated along the segment and pitched with the level, plus a `WaterVolume` with `FluidVelocity` = the current. The pawn's built-in swim mode keys off the tag; the velocity is for rigidbodies (boats later). **Bed colour** (Mark, same day: "see-through but not totally clear"): a palette-blue bed under refracting water read as solid teal, so near chunks now paint the river bed as the biome ground darkened like wet soil (`TerrainChunkBiomeDisplay.RiverBedColor`, `riverBedAsGround`, default true) and the water (`MurkDistance` 360 u ≈ 35 % tint at a 4 m bed) shows it through; lakes stay palette-blue (no surface yet) and the far tier passes `riverBedAsGround: false` so its river creases, which carry no strip, stay blue as the 2026-10-07 "keep that area blue" asked. The water strip is mitred at every segment join (bisector sides), so bends no longer open a sliver of bed between quads, and every segment's quad AND trigger belong to exactly one chunk — the one holding the segment midpoint (quads built in every chunk a segment touched drew border segments twice: two coplanar refracting surfaces, z-fight + double tint). Screenshot caveat: the editor's `camera_screenshot` render shows the river as flat teal at every angle, even straight down over a 1.6 m bed, so transparency can only be judged in the game window.

**Clear + reflective (Mark, same day: "the water should be clear and reflective, reflect the sky and trees, but not always a mirror — depends on the light"):** `riverwater.shader` is now refraction (frame-buffer copy, `MurkDistance` 1600 u = 40 m, so a 4 m bed shows at ~10 % tint) mixed with a reflection by Schlick Fresnel (`FresnelF0` 0.02: looking down you see the bed, grazing angles mirror; `ReflectionStrength` scales it). The reflection is a screen-space trace the water runs for itself (`ScreenSpace::Trace`, the engine's hierarchical depth-chain march — the engine's own SSR pass only handles opaque surfaces and skipped the translucent water, so `DynamicReflections::Sample` was always empty): where the reflected ray lands on screen (trees, banks, clouds, the dome) the frame-buffer copy is mirrored, fading at the screen edges; anywhere else a procedural sky from the SkyDome's `SkyZenith` / `SkyHorizon` / `SkySunGlow` / `SunDirection`, which `SkyDome.Apply` now also publishes scene-wide (renderer attributes only reached the dome's own draw) with `SkyDomeActive` 1; with no dome publishing (terrainTest's cycle object is disabled) the fallback is the flat `NoDomeSkyColor`. The sun glint is the standard specular. terrainTest's FlyCamera carries a **ScreenSpaceReflections** post-process so the depth chain the trace reads is downsampled. The reflected ray follows only `ReflectionRippleWobble` (0.15) of the ripple tilt — tracing along the full ripple normal scattered the rays into a speckled, broken mirror at grazing angles; lighting and refraction still use the full ripple normal.

**Simplified (Mark, same day, with a screenshot of a glitter field over teal with wavy lines: "REALLY simplified … where a tree would reflect, show the riverbed through the water in the tree's silhouette … under no circumstances wavy W lines or a blue that appears nowhere else in the game"):** the shader no longer has a water colour. What you see is the frame-buffer copy of the bed, only darkened by distance through the water (`AbsorbTint` × `ClearDistance`, 40 m) — `ShallowColor` / `DeepColor` are gone; the foam streaks are gone; `RippleStrength` 0.03 and roughness 0.2 turn the sparkle field into a soft sun band. The reflection is the sky alone (dome colours → gradient fog colour `g_vGradientFogColor_Opacity` → camera background), mixed by Fresnel, and the depth-chain trace is now only a **mask**: a hit (tree, bank) removes the sky reflection there so the bed shows through in the silhouette's shape; nothing is mirrored in colour. First look: the Clover bed through 40 m-clear water was a light green sheet indistinguishable from the bank, so `RiverBedColor` went to a darkened mud, then (Mark: "more grey or light brown, sandy-ish") to a fixed sand `RiverBedSand` (0.60, 0.54, 0.44) with a fifth of the biome ground mixed in, and `ClearDistance` to 480 u (12 m, `AbsorbTint` 0.45/0.62/0.66): a 4 m bed darkens about a third, a 1 m shallow stays almost clear. Absorption is a true Beer-Lambert `pow(AbsorbTint, dist / ClearDistance)`, not a lerp: at a grazing view the "through the water" sample's path to the far bank is tens of metres, and with the capped lerp the tree silhouettes showed the far bank's bright grass; now that path goes dark. `ReflectionRippleWobble` 0.05 — the silhouette mask dithered at grazing angles with more. A hard "no reflection where the ray hits" mask then showed every gap between leaves as a black/white speckle (foliage cards are half holes), a luminance-scaled version still snapped to coarse depth-mip blocks at grazing angles (the hierarchical march's hit cells), and a pure Beer-Lambert path went to black on long grazing paths. **Final (2026-10-21):** no screen-space trace at all — the reflection is the sky alone by Fresnel, nothing on the banks is mirrored, and what absorption removes is replaced by the sky's light scattered in the water (`InScatter` 0.45 × the sky straight up), so deep water tends to a dark sky, never black, and still no colour the scene does not already have. `ScreenSpaceTrace.hlsl` and the FlyCamera's `ScreenSpaceReflections` component are gone with it. Bank / tree silhouettes in the water remain an open idea (needs a planar reflection or an SSR pass that includes translucents). Mark, seeing the 12 m / full-Fresnel version from a low fly-cam: "grey and not see-through at all" — so `ClearDistance` 2000 u (50 m: a 30 m grazing path still keeps ~70 %), `AbsorbTint` 0.55/0.70/0.75, `InScatter` 0.25 and `ReflectionStrength` 0.5 (even at grazing angles only half the sky comes back); see-through at any angle is the priority over a physically strong grazing mirror. **Why it was still flat grey at low views even then:** the surface was one strip per chunk, and translucents draw back to front with a frame-buffer copy per draw — looking along the river, a far chunk's strip was already in the near strip's copy wherever the two overlapped on screen, so every low pixel refracted water-behind-water-behind-water and converged on the sky colour (the same mechanism made the very first versions "flat teal at every angle", which I had blamed on the capture path). Now `TerrainWorldManager.RiverWater.cs` keeps ONE merged surface mesh for all loaded chunks' segments (`_riverSegmentsByChunk`, rebuilt by `TickRiverSurface` at most every 0.25 s when a chunk with river loads or unloads; `DestroyChunk` → `ReleaseRiverWater`, `UnloadAll` → `DestroyRiverSurface`), so the whole river is one draw and one copy of the opaque scene; the per-segment swim volumes stay under their chunk. **The real cause of every "flat grey / flat teal at any angle" (found after the merge changed nothing):** `Depth::GetNormalized` reads the engine's depth chain (`DepthChainDownsample`), which only exists while a `ScreenSpaceReflections` pass is on the camera — the bed showed through exactly while that component was on the FlyCamera and never otherwise; without it every depth read said "far", the murk went to 1 and the water became its tint. The shader therefore reads **no depth buffer at all**: the merged mesh has three vertices across each segment end (bank, centre line, bank), vertex colour R carries the carved water depth under the centre line (`level − TerrainPreviewPipeline.Sample(...)`, 0 at the banks, 8-bit over 8 m), and the shader stretches that depth by the view angle for the Beer-Lambert path. Along-river UVs are metres modulo 64 (vertex UVs are half precision — raw arc metres stepped by whole metres) with every along-river ripple frequency a whole number of cycles per 64 m, so the wrap is seamless. **No shading model:** `ShadingModelStandard::Shade` added its own indirect specular with a Fresnel that reaches 100 % at grazing angles whatever the material asked for — a grey-blue sheen over the whole river at low views ("it's grey, it should be completely clear"). The water now outputs `lerp(bed-through-water, sky, fresnel × ReflectionStrength 0.35)` plus an explicit glint of the dome's sun on the ripples (`SunGlintPower` 300 / `SunGlintStrength`), then fog. Clear distance 80 m, `InScatter` 0.15. **Valheim-clear + wake (Mark, same day: "white hue … I want it CLEAR like Valheim, with some white wake sort of things"):** the white was the overcast dome coming back through `ReflectionStrength` and `InScatter`, so those are 0.15 and 0.05 and `AbsorbTint` 0.78/0.86/0.88 — the bed is what you see. On top, `GetWake`: sparse white streaks from a hashed value noise (cells `WakeLengthMeters` 8 × `WakeWidthMeters` 1.2, so they are long along the flow and thin across, wrapped to the 64 m tile) under a slower patch envelope, thresholded (`WakeThreshold` 0.74) and carried downstream, plus bank foam where the vertex depth is under `EdgeFoamDepth01` of the range; `WakeStrength` 0.5 lerps toward the foam colour — white lit by the dome's zenith (`skyUp × 1.15`), so at night the foam is as dark as the rest; `WakeThreshold` 0.72 with the envelope scaling the noise 0.5–1.0 (0.86 showed no streaks at all at noon). Tuned by eye over river 24 at noon / dusk / night via `cycle_day`. Hashed noise, never sine bands — the "W" lines were the thing Mark rejected. **Valheim proper (Mark, same day, with Valheim screenshots: "in complete daylight there's no reflections… I want it to look like this"):** Valheim's water is clear at your feet and a mirror of the sky toward the far bank, with the sun's glitter streak — i.e. true Fresnel, not "no reflection". So `ReflectionStrength` is back to 1.0 (F0 0.02: 2 % straight down, the whole sky at grazing angles), `SunGlintStrength` 2.0, and the mirrored sky is now the **dome's own sky**: `SkyReflection` is a port of `sky_dome.shader`'s MainPs (horizon→zenith gradient, sun-side band, halo, both cloud layers from the same `sky_clouds.png` on the same ceiling projection, silver lining, horizon fade; stars skipped), so the clouds in the river are the clouds overhead. `SkyDome` now publishes `CloudLit / CloudShade / CloudCover` (Apply) and `CloudOffset / CloudScale` (OnUpdate) on `Scene.RenderAttributes` next to the colours; `riverwater.vmat` carries `CloudNoise "materials/environment/sky/sky_clouds.png"`. The earlier "grey" complaints were the depth-chain bug and the shading model, not the reflection itself — with those gone, full Fresnel over a real sky is the look. Foam is white at the sky's *brightness* (luminance of the zenith, 20 % sky tint) — tinted by the zenith colour it came out blue. Verified through `sbox-cli --call` (the harness MCP was down; the editor was relaunched per the standing permission): noon over river 24 the bed shows through, and from the bank at 4° the river is a rippled mirror of the overcast dome with sand under the near water. **Ends + foam (Mark, same day, two screenshots: "no water at all at the start of the river" and "very shitty flow lines"):** the carve rounds each river end with a half-width cap (point distance past the end vertex) but the strip stopped dead at the end vertex, so a source was a dry carved bowl behind a straight water edge — the surface builder now pushes an open end out by the half width (what pokes past the channel lies under the bank). The hashed value-noise wake (long thin cells) read as straight bars, so foam is now `GetFoam`: the dome's tileable cloud noise sampled in metres (`FoamScaleMeters` 8, a whole number of tiles per 64 m), a slower larger sample gating it into sparse groups, a wide smoothstep band (a narrow one on the 8-bit noise drew contour outlines), plus shore foam where the vertex depth is shallow — organic blobs drifting with the current, never bars or bands. `terrain_tp_river N source|mouth` parks the camera close over a river end. **terrainTest's DayNightCycle object now ships enabled** (Mark: "it should be running the short daylight cycle"; the flat default sky looked wrong and gave the water nothing to mirror) — `cycle_toggle` still switches it off. Voxel note: the carve lives in `TerrainPreviewPipeline.Sample` as a function of (x, y) and the water rig is built from the river network, not the mesh, so a voxel density field can evaluate the same carve and keep the same water.

## Redwood ledges, round two (2026-10-07)

**Face slope**: the face width is the larger of **Redwood Ledge Face Width (m)** and height ÷ tan(**Redwood Ledge Face Slope (deg)**, 70°), so a 30 m ledge is an 11 m face the 2 m grid resolves over rows instead of a 1 m step the vertex snapping had to fake — the fins and "spiky" bands were that snapping failing on tall ledges; snapping now only touches faces ≤ 2.5 m wide (ledges under ~7 m). **Notch streets fixed** (the actual cause of the remaining spikes, found with `terrain_probe`: the field, gradient and phase were smooth, the ramp mask jumped 0→1→0 inside two metres): each street cell hashed its own width, so the mask stepped at every cell boundary — a wall across the notch and alternating vertices; widths now blend along the street between neighbouring cells, and the notch banks are at least half the ledge height wide so a 30 m ledge's walk-up keeps ~50° sides. The real killer was the street GRID spacing being multiplied by the height-dependent notch scale: far from the origin a small height drift slid the pattern by whole periods, so the mask flickered per vertex. The grid spacing is now fixed in world space (**Redwood Ledge Ramp Spacing (m)** as written); only the notch width scales with height. **Basin suppression**: a contour closing around a LOW made an inward-facing ring — a sinkhole with a cliff wall; the drop now fades where the swell field's Laplacian (10 m probes, ledge-line octaves) times 250 m exceeds the slope (bowl tighter than ~250 m), so ledges only nest around highs.

## Distant terrain tier (2026-10-07)

## Spawn solve (replaces Valley Auto)

On **Generate** when **World - Solve Spawn On Generate** is on:

1. Build land biomes once per seed.
2. Sample lake mask once; quantile threshold; compute **Lakes - Mask Offset X/Y** from wet centroid + dry-spawn nudge (no spiral search).
3. Apply water mask once; validate coverage, dry spawn, **Lakes - Showcase Water Radius (m)**.
4. Reject seed only if dry spawn or coverage constraints fail after that single offset (+ small post-build nudge if needed).

**Showcase water** (informational): nearest **open lake** shoreline within **Lakes - Showcase Water Radius (m)** of spawn. Does not fail the solve — status line reports distance.

## Runtime world generation (`terraintest.scene`)

Editor **Generate** and runtime meshes share **`TerrainPreviewPipeline.Sample()`** — no PNG is read back for height.

| Step | What happens |
|------|----------------|
| 1. Tune | Terrain Noise Preview tool opens on the scene world's recipe (`WorldSaves/<WorldName>/world.json`, label "Editing: world recipe"). **Generate** still writes a bundle under `Assets/terrain/preview/` for the record; **Apply To World Recipe** writes the settings into world.json and marks the saved biome map stale. **Load World Recipe** throws your edits away and reloads it. |
| 2. Play | `TerrainWorldManager` with **World - Settings Source** = **World Recipe First** (default since 2026-10-08) generates from that recipe. The minimap / inspector map is `biome_map.png` next to it, stamped (`biome_map.json`) with the fingerprint of the settings that baked it; a stamp that does not match the live settings = stale → rebuilt in the background on Play (512 px) or at full resolution with Regenerate. In edit mode the inspector rebuilds a stale map by itself, budgeted per frame at 1024 px (`EditorAutoBakeMaxResolution`), so you can look at what the recipe would spawn before pressing Play. Two traps fixed the same day: the manager caches its resolved settings, so a recipe edited on disk did not change the fingerprint — `RefreshSavedBiomeMapStaleState` now hashes the raw world.json text and drops the cache when it changes; and `Texture.LoadFromFileSystem` is path-cached, so re-reading biome_map.png right after a bake returned the OLD image — the HUD / inspector now bind the in-memory texture of the last bake and only read the PNG when there is none. Console says `Saved biome map is stale — editor rebake…` and `Biome map rebuilt —` so a missing refresh can be traced. **Rivers on maps** (2026-10-08): rivers are 8–28 m wide and a map pixel is 40–80 m, so per-pixel sampling never showed them; `TerrainRiverMapOverlay.Stamp` draws every segment as a ≥1.5 px water-coloured line on the manager map, the inspector raster and the tool's Biomes / Water / Lakes layers. Before this, tool, minimap and terrain could show three different worlds (Mark 2026-10-08). |
| 3. Stream | Forward-cone + side-square streaming. **Stream Build Budget (ms)** spreads mesh work per frame; near chunks sync on turn (capped); distant chunks use coarser LOD. |
| 4. Persist | First play writes `WorldSaves/<WorldName>/world.json` with full `PreviewSettings`. Runtime also stores **`dayNumber`** (starts at 1; +1 each dawn via `EnvironmentDayNightCycle`). |

**World - Override World Scalars From Component** — off (default): seed, diameter, height, ocean ring, and lake offsets match the tuned bundle (same as PNG). Turn on only to force component inspector values.

**World - Run Lake Spawn Solve On Load** — only for **Component Defaults Only** source. Tuned bundles already include solved **Lakes - Mask Offset X/Y**.

Biome-specific sculpting (`TerrainPreviewBiomeTerrainShaper`, etc.) runs in the shared pipeline — tune under **Biome Terrain** tab; re-Generate, then play.

**Clover Hills (current):** Voronoi cells (~**400 m** spacing) pick candidate centers. **Hill Density** decides which cells become big hills (seeded); skipped cells form **low plateau / gap floors**. Peaks SoftCap at **`BiomeCloverMaxHeightMeters`** (default **100 m**). **Surface bumps are applied after SoftCap** (and land-display ceiling includes micro headroom) so they are not clipped off plateaus — smooth FBM only (no hash-cell steps: those aliased into vertical spikes on the 2 m vertex grid), with a slow envelope varying bump amount across the biome (0.35–1.3× amp) and a gain + soft-clip so bumps actually reach the configured ±amplitude (raw FBM averages toward flat). The main layer is domain-warped so bumps cluster instead of reading as even noise blobs. Defaults ~**±1.5 m** at ~**24 m** wavelength plus a ripple at ~0.4× wavelength and a dense ~9 m detail layer (old JSON 0 → those defaults; wavelengths floored at 8 m). Tune via **Biome Terrain - Clover Micro Wavelength (m)** / **Clover Micro Amplitude (m)**.

**Redwood Forest (current, `TerrainPreviewBiomeTerrainShaper.Redwood.cs`):** a continuously **sloped hillside** instead of Clover's flat-topped cells — domain-warped macro swells (**Biome Shape - Redwood Hillside Wavelength (m)** / **Redwood Hillside Relief (m)**, defaults 600 m / 170 m → median slope ≈ 12°, p90 ≈ 23°, steep stretches 25–40°) plus rounded gullies (**Redwood Ravine Wavelength (m)** / **Redwood Ravine Depth (m)**) and a small duff layer (**Redwood Micro Amplitude (m)**, shared grit helper with Clover). The hillside rides on the world base and SoftCaps at `BiomeRedwoodMaxHeightMeters`; ledges and duff are added **after** the cap. **Ledges:** contour lines of the real pre-ledge ground - world base + hillside swells, soft-capped, **without** the ravine creases and with only the first **Redwood Ledge Line Detail (octaves)** (default 2 of the hillside's 4) of the swell noise, so the lines are long smooth nested sweeps like contour lines on a topo map (Mark's sketch, 2026-10-06; set 4 to follow every swell) - every **Redwood Ledge Rise Interval (m)** of climb (2026-10-06: they used to be contours of the hillside noise alone, so where the world base out-sloped the hillside a ledge raised the downhill side and faced the wrong way; and the ravines' V creases kinked every line at each gully - now a gully simply cuts across a bench; three base samples per vertex, chunk build only) (default 70 m since 2026-10-06, Mark: not so close together → one ledge line every ~330 m of ground on a 12° slope; steeper ground gets them closer, flats none) become a sharp drop whose height varies along the line between **Redwood Ledge Height Min (m)** and **Max (m)** (defaults 3–30 m since 2026-10-06, Mark: twice as tall, slow noise at **Redwood Ledge Height Wavelength (m)**, tall stretches rarer; capped at 85 % of the rise interval so the ground between ledges never tilts back downhill). The face is a hard step **Redwood Ledge Face Width (m)** wide (1 m in the sampled field). **Mesh snapping — REMOVED 2026-10-07** (every fin and spike along a ledge came from it; faces are now sloped at **Redwood Ledge Face Slope (deg)** and resolved by plain heightfield rows; the ledge-aware diagonal choice stays). Historical description: the sample carries `TerrainBiomeLedgeInfo` (signed distance, uphill direction, half face width — only deep inside Redwood and only where the drop is sharp), and every interior chunk vertex within one grid step of a ledge line is slid horizontally onto the line and re-sampled just past it on its own side, so the two vertices straddling the drop share an XY and the face is a true vertical quad that follows the real line instead of a 2 m staircase (verified by collider traces: the drop stays inside 1 m across consecutive rows). Chunk-border vertices are never moved (a neighbour of another LOD would not match). A border vertex whose sample lies inside the face, and any interior vertex that has to stay on its grid spot, takes the height of its own terrace at that spot (`SettleBorderVerticesOnTheirTerrace`, 2026-10-06 — the half-way heights they used to keep were the specks along a line). Two things stop the foot of the cliff from growing spikes: **ledge-aware triangulation** (`UseLedgeAwareAltDiagonal` — the sample reports which side of the line every near-line vertex is on, and a quad whose line clips one corner gets the diagonal that avoids that corner, so the corner becomes a vertical triangle on the line instead of a slanted fin into the other terrace; harness: 306 → 29 fins per 800 m square) and **fold repair** (`RepairFoldedQuads` — where the hillside gradient turns sharply two neighbours can be slid apart and invert a triangle; the worst-moved vertex is put back on its grid spot). Only sharp drops (face ≤ 2.5 m) are snapped; notches and blends are slopes the grid follows anyway. A snapped vertex whose re-sample comes back on the other terrace (an upper vertex lower than it was, or a lower one higher - the line curved) is left on its grid spot instead of hanging a spike off the foot of the cliff. Normals come from actual neighbour positions so the face shades sideways. Dev: `terrain_dump_chunk [x y]` writes the chunk under the fly camera as v/f text to the data folder, `terrain_ledge_diagonals 0|1` toggles the triangulation for A/B dumps. One extra pipeline sample per snapped vertex, chunk build only. The terrace offset is zero-mean (±height/2 either side of the line) so caps and mean elevation are untouched, and the hillside simply resumes above the drop. **Passable places, two kinds:** (1) walk-up notches — two perpendicular families of seed-rotated, gently warped ramp "streets" (**Redwood Ledge Ramp Spacing (m)** grid, **Redwood Ledge Ramp Width (m)** notch, widths vary 0.6–1.4× per notch; both scale up to 2.5× with ledge height so notch sides stay ~50° gully banks) stretch the drop into a *local* notch — never more than 45 % of the gap to the next ledge — that adds **Redwood Ledge Ramp Grade (deg)** on top of the hillside, so any ledge has a walk-up within ~1.4× its spacing (defaults 28 / 6 / 12° → typically 10 m, worst ≈ 45 m for 5 m ledges, ~100 m for 15 m ones); (2) **blend stretches** — slow-noise patches (**Redwood Ledge Blend Coverage (0–1)**, **Redwood Ledge Blend Length (m)**, defaults 0.3 / 120 m ≈ 8–16 % of each ledge) where the drop height fades to zero over ~50 m and the ground is plain hillside, the drive-up places. **Rule learned the hard way:** the terrace offset is a sawtooth with the ledge height as amplitude, so the ground between two ledges depends on that height; anything that changes it (notches, blends, height noise) must do so over tens of metres, or every edge becomes a wall — stretching the step across the whole gap produced a waffle grid of walls along the street and blend edges. On near-flat ground (< ~6 % grade) the drop softens by itself so a closed contour loop can never leave an unclimbable knob. Ledge lines sit on the steeper ground by construction (more contours per metre there), so a ramp or blend is typically a 20–30° slope — tune **Redwood Hillside Relief (m)** if vehicles need gentler ground. Cost: two extra hillside samples per vertex for the contour gradient — chunk build only. Calibrated in a standalone .NET harness (slope percentiles, ledge spacing, BFS walk-to-ramp distance).



**Sample cost (QC 2026-10-05):** `Pipeline.Sample()` is the streaming cost — 33² calls per 64 m chunk. Rules that keep it honest: the mask / field / falloff / slope previews are **editor PNG modes only** (`TerrainPreviewGenerator` computes them directly; the runtime sample never pays for them — the mountain speck-filter mask alone was ~60 % of a mountain-core sample); the mountain ridge frame is a pure function of (settings, nx, ny) and is memoised per thread in `TerrainPreviewMountainSpawnMask` (one sample used to rebuild it up to eight times); `TerrainPreviewLandDiskFields.BeginSample` verifies the raster caches once per sample (the shore / coastal / water lookups then skip their own fingerprint checks); coastal personality noise is skipped beyond the widest possible shore fade. Measured: the `terrain_tp_biome` 19,200-sample scan went 580 → 348 ms with bit-identical heights. Keep new per-sample work gated the same way.


**Fast sculpt iteration:** Chunk meshes call **`Sample()`** for height — the biome PNG is **display-only**. Leave **Regenerate Biome Map On Play** off on `TerrainWorldManager` so Play only builds nearby chunks with current math (reuse saved HUD/biome PNG). Re-run editor **Generate** only when biome *placement*/lakes/seed must change; sculpt-only tweaks do not need a full Generate for playtest.

**Mountains:** `lowlandMeters = rolling base + sculpt` → `mountainMeters = lowland + peakBoost × headroom` → **`lerp(a, b, smoothstep(influence))`**. No ridged slope pass. Re-Generate after tuning.

**Playtest (`terraintest.scene`):** fly camera **Follow terrain height** optional. Press **J** to spawn a scale-reference player at the camera. Console **`terrain_tp_biome <clover|redwood|amber|mountain> [cliff] [minStepMeters]`** (`Code/World/Terrain/TerrainDevCommands.cs`) spiral-scans the live generation settings from the world centre for the nearest land deep inside that biome (≥ 90 % weight; with `cliff`: the nearest spot where the ground steps ≥ minStep m within 4 m) and parks the fly camera above it — the quickest way to inspect a Redwood ledge. `perf_trees 0` hides trees for a clear look. Chunk meshes: **33 verts/side** (~2 m on 64 m chunks); **Height Smooth Passes = 0** keeps natural slopes between corners. Shore blend eases land toward sea level near lakes and the outer rim.

**Mesh LOD:** distance-based both ways with hysteresis — chunks outside the high-priority radius build at **Far Stream Vertices Per Side** (17 ≈ 4 m), promote in place to full detail inside **High-Priority Radius**, and demote back only beyond radius + **LOD Demote Margin (chunks)** so boundary chunks never flip-flop. LOD distance is **3D** (altitude vs each chunk's cached ground height counts; climbing demotes the ground below, diving promotes it; refreshes also trigger on ~48 m altitude change), while **colliders and entity population stay on horizontal range** — physics under a falling player never switches off with height. The initial load uses the same distance rule (no all-full-detail start). Every chunk, any LOD, snaps its **border verts onto the far-LOD lattice**, so full↔far seams are watertight by construction — no neighbor tracking, no tears in either direction. Far Stream Vertices Per Side must keep (full−1) divisible by (far−1) (e.g. 33/17) or border snapping turns off.

**Vegetation (trees):** `TerrainVegetationScatter` runs once when a chunk loads (parented under the chunk GO; local meters match mesh verts). Large FBM picks **forest vs clearing**; inside forest, hash picks one prefab from the biome's list (Clover: the current `environment_elm1–6_v<N>`; Redwood: elm3 / elm6 of the same set — the old `temp_tree` / `propertree` / redwood models were deleted). Clover also runs a **sapling layer** (`environment_elmsapling1–5_v14`) on its own grid, **full-detail chunks only** (far chunks get theirs when promoted), weighted toward forest edges by **Vegetation - Clover — Sapling Forest Edge Bias (0–1)** and kept **Sapling Trunk Clearance (m)** away from large trunks. Trees and saplings lean with the ground: **Slope Tilt Influence (0–1)** × slope angle, capped at **Max Slope Tilt (deg)** (default 0.5 / 15°; two extra height samples per tree, chunk load only). Tree / prop colliders AND shadows are switched on and off with the chunk's terrain collider (**Collision Range**), so far render-only chunks carry no tree physics or shadow casters; saplings never cast shadows. Tree models carry their own LOD chain (`LODGroupList` in the vmdl, written by `Blender/scripts/create_elm_tree.py` `TREE_LODS` / `create_elm_sapling.py` `SAPLING_LODS`). Clover also scatters rock/stick **clusters**, plus a tunable chance (default **5%**) to place one stick in open ground beside each large tree. Stream camera positions must be converted engine→meters before `RefreshChunks` (same as `TryRefreshStreamChunks`) or FinishWorldLoad will unload every chunk and wipe trees.

**Biome edges:** **Biomes - Continuous Placement At Sample** makes blobby patches. **Biomes - Edge Color Blend (0–1)** (~0.35) softens borders only — interiors stay dominant biome color. Shader splats can replace this later.

**Azure Coast:** **Biomes - Azure Coast** replaces other land biomes with teal on dry land within **Biomes - Azure Coast Width (m)** world meters of display-water shores (blue lakes + rim ocean), **Biomes - Azure Coast Min Distance From Spawn (m)**+ only. Hard biome edge (no coverage fade vs other land); inland band end tapers at ~15° along the coast so tips narrow instead of a blunt 90° cutoff from a constant-width offset.

## Performance benchmark (terrainTest)

`TerrainPerfBench` (`Code/World/Terrain/TerrainPerfBench.cs`) is a repeatable FPS check for terrain / vegetation changes. In terrainTest, with the fly camera active (not an L-spawned pawn), run `perf_bench <label>` in the console. It drives the camera over a fixed route: 12 s eye-height 360° spins at three stations (0,0 / 900,350 / -700,-600 m), then a 50 s, 30 m-high flight from x -1400 to +1400 m. Each phase waits for chunk streaming to settle first. It logs avg FPS, 1 % low, p50/p95/p99, worst frame, hitches over 33 / 50 ms and chunk / tree / sapling / prop counts, and writes JSON to `FileSystem.Data` `perf/` (`terrain_latest.json` plus a timestamped copy). `perf_bench_baseline` promotes the latest run to `perf/terrain_baseline.json`; later runs print deltas against it. `perf_bench_stop` aborts. Keep the window size, graphics settings and FPS cap the same between runs.

A/B switches for the benchmark (or plain play, runtime only): `perf_trees 0|1` hides / shows all terrain scatter (trees, saplings, rocks, sticks; new chunks do not scatter while hidden), `perf_shadows 0|1` turns sun / moon shadows off / on (`EnvironmentDayNightCycle` honours it), `perf_aimassist 0|1` switches the local pawn's grapple aim assist (per-frame trace spray into nearby trees), `perf_player hud|body|anim 0|1` switches one part of the local pawn off to bisect what the pawn costs, and `perf_watch 1|0` logs one line per second (frame avg / worst, chunk loads / remeshes / near-flips, camera switch / movement, sun state) to catch periodic flicker or hitches. Reports record the tree / shadow states.
